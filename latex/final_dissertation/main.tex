\begin{abstract}
This dissertation asks whether a reinforcement learning agent can trade a
stock-market fund more profitably than simply buying and holding it.
Markets make this question treacherous: there is no ground truth, so a
broken implementation can look like a mediocre strategy indefinitely. The
methodology is therefore understanding-first. Two learning algorithms,
REINFORCE and Deep Q-learning, are derived step by step and implemented
from scratch; the library algorithm PPO is added as a third comparison.
Each agent sees a nine-feature state, justified feature by feature against
explicit admissibility criteria, and chooses daily between holding, buying
a fixed money slice of the asset, or selling one. The reward is the change
in portfolio wealth after fees. Evaluation uses eight years of S\&P~500
data under a strict chronological split. All design choices are settled on
a validation year, and 24 held-out months are then read exactly once,
across six seeds and four reference strategies.

No learned agent beats buy-and-hold, on raw return or risk-adjusted
return. A purpose-built diagnostic explains why. REINFORCE converges, in
all six seeds, to policies that ignore the state entirely and respond only
to which actions are legal; PPO does the same. Deep Q-learning does use
its state, but earns only 41\% of the benchmark's return for 40\% of its
drawdown: it reduces risk by holding less, not by timing the market.
Enriching the state from one feature to twelve changes nothing beyond seed
noise. This null result is the predicted consequence of the objective:
under a risk-neutral wealth reward on a rising asset the best policy is to
always buy, so a richer state has nothing to add. A drawdown penalty makes
matters worse, because not trading is the cheapest way to avoid drawdown.

The most consequential contribution is methodological. An audit of the
project's own earlier code found twelve defects, three severe enough to
invalidate results. One was a ``buy-and-hold'' benchmark that in fact
deployed capital a tenth at a time; another was a trade size that made the
benchmark unreachable by any policy. Correcting them replaced an apparent
victory with a measurable, explainable shortfall. The dissertation
therefore delivers a traceable pipeline from derivation to implementation,
a test for whether a trained policy uses its observations at all, and an
evaluation framework under which a negative result is informative rather
than merely disappointing.
\end{abstract}
