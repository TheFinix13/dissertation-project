\begin{abstract}
Reinforcement learning (RL) promises trading agents that learn from
experience, but markets provide no ground truth against which a broken
implementation can be detected. This dissertation adopts an
understanding-first methodology: the two canonical RL algorithm families
--- the Monte-Carlo policy gradient (REINFORCE) and value-based Deep
Q-learning --- are derived from first principles, implemented from
scratch, validated on games with known outcomes, and only then applied to
a carefully formulated trading MDP with a complete 9-feature observable
state, a divisible-asset action model and a fee-inclusive wealth-change
reward. A three-algorithm ablation (adding library PPO) and a fee
sensitivity study complete the evaluation, conducted on 26 held-out
months of S\&P~500 ETF data against properly capitalised baselines.
The policy-gradient agent converges to a policy identical to buy-and-hold
on every test month --- shown to be the rational optimum under a
risk-neutral wealth objective --- while Deep Q-learning finds a nearby
policy with lower drawdown (4.8\% vs.\ 6.0\%) and higher monthly Sharpe
ratio (0.45 vs.\ 0.40) at negligible return cost, and reduces its trading
intensity threefold as fees rise. PPO proves bimodal across seeds,
landing on either rational optimum. A pilot study demonstrates that with
a minimal state the collapse to buy-and-hold is universal across
policy-gradient optimisers, and that without a fully invested baseline it
would be misreported as a ten-fold victory over the market. The
dissertation contributes a fully traceable derivation-to-implementation
pipeline, an honest evaluation framework for trading RL, and an empirical
characterisation of how the algorithm families respond differently to the
same objective.
\end{abstract}
