\paragraph{Why the log appears --- one-paragraph summary for the viva.}
The objective $J(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{\tau\sim p_\theta}[G(\tau)]$
depends on $\theta$ only through the sampling distribution. Its gradient,
$\int \nabla_\theta p_\theta(\tau) G(\tau)\,d\tau$, is not an expectation
and cannot be estimated from rollouts --- until the identity
$\nabla_\theta p_\theta = p_\theta \nabla_\theta \log p_\theta$ rewrites
it as
$\mathbb{E}_{\tau}[\nabla_\theta \log p_\theta(\tau)\, G(\tau)]$. Taking
the log of the trajectory probability factorises it into a sum in which
the unknown market dynamics are additive constants with respect to
$\theta$ and vanish under the gradient, leaving only
$\sum_t \nabla_\theta \log \pi_\theta(a_t|s_t)$. Every step is exact; the
only approximation is replacing the expectation by an average over $N$
sampled trajectories. The implemented loss is the negative of that
average, because PyTorch minimises.
