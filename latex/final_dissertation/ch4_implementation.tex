\chapter{System Design \& Implementation}
\label{ch:implementation}

This chapter describes how the mathematical formulation of Chapter~3 is
realised in code. The guiding requirement, set explicitly by the
supervision process, is that every implemented line be traceable to a
stated equation: the policy-gradient loss in the code \emph{is}
Equation~\ref{eq:reinforce_loss}; the temporal-difference loss
\emph{is} Equation~\ref{eq:dqn_loss}. Both learning algorithms are
implemented from scratch in PyTorch \cite{paszke2019} with the Adam
optimiser \cite{kingma2015adam}; environments follow the Gymnasium API
\cite{brockman2016gym, towers2024gymnasium}. No reinforcement-learning
library performs any part of the final-model training.

\section{Architecture overview}
\label{sec:arch}

The system separates six concerns, each in its own module:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Feature registry} (\texttt{features.py}): the single
        definition of every feature, its canonical ordering, the rolling
        window lengths, and the ladder of Section~\ref{sec:state_rationale}
        expressed as named subsets. Nothing else in the codebase decides
        what a state contains.
  \item \textbf{Data pipeline} (\texttt{data.py}): downloads OHLCV bars,
        computes market features on the continuous series, cuts monthly
        episodes and applies the three-way chronological split; run once and
        cached to disk.
  \item \textbf{Environment} (\texttt{env.py}): a Gymnasium
        \texttt{gym.Env} implementing the MDP --- state assembly, trade
        execution, fees, masking, termination and the admissibility guard.
  \item \textbf{Agents} (\texttt{agents.py}): the two from-scratch
        learners, exposing a common
        \texttt{policy(obs, mask) $\to$ action} interface after training,
        plus a thin adapter that gives the third-party PPO the same
        interface.
  \item \textbf{Reference strategies and rollout}
        (\texttt{baselines.py}, \texttt{rollout.py}): the four benchmarks
        and the single episode-runner and metric summariser they share with
        the learned agents.
  \item \textbf{Experiment harness} (\texttt{harness.py}) and the four
        scripts it drives: \texttt{calibrate\_slice.py} sizes the action
        model, \texttt{run\_conditioning.py} diagnoses the variance of the
        measurement, \texttt{run\_ladder.py} runs the saturation study on
        validation, and \texttt{run\_final.py} spends the test months. Each
        writes a JSON record of the configuration, per-month results,
        diagnostics and provenance.
\end{enumerate}

Two properties of this arrangement do methodological work rather than
merely organising code. Because baselines, learned agents and diagnostics
all flow through the same \texttt{run\_episode} function, no method can be
advantaged by a differing evaluation path. And because both experiment
scripts obtain their environments from the same \texttt{harness.Config}
object, any difference between two reported rows must appear in the
configuration dictionary stored alongside them; there is no route by which
two rows could differ in a way the results file does not record.
Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline} summarises the flow.

\section{Data pipeline}
\label{sec:data}

Daily auto-adjusted OHLCV bars for SPY (the S\&P~500 ETF) covering
January 2018 to December 2025 are retrieved via the \texttt{yfinance} API.
SPY is chosen deliberately: it is among the most liquid instruments in the
world, so the frictionless-execution assumption of the environment (trades
fill at the closing price) is least violated, and as a broad index fund it
carries no single-company idiosyncrasies. The full open, high, low, close
and volume record is retained rather than the closing price alone, because
the daily high and low are what make range-based risk representable at all
(Section~\ref{sec:state_def}).

\paragraph{Features are computed before episodes are cut.} The pipeline
computes every market feature on the continuous price series and only then
slices it into months. This ordering is the whole point of the module.
Computing a 20-day trend reference inside a monthly episode would leave the
first day of every month with no history to average, so the agent would be
told the market has no trend and no volatility at precisely the moment it
chooses its opening position. Momentum does not reset because a new month
has begun.

Making that ordering work requires one further step. A rolling window
needs history \emph{before} the first day it is reported for, so the
pipeline fetches a warm-up lead from September 2017, computes features
across the join, and only then discards the lead. The longest window in
use is 20 bars, so \texttt{WARMUP\_BARS} is 21 and the pipeline refuses to
proceed if fewer lead bars are available than that. It also asserts that no
missing value survives into any retained episode, which is the check that
would catch a silently shortened lead.

\paragraph{Episodes and the split.} Days are grouped into calendar months
and months with fewer than 15 trading days are dropped, giving 96 monthly
episodes. These are split chronologically and on calendar boundaries
(Figure~\ref{fig:split}): 60 training months (2018--2022), 12 validation
months (2023) and 24 test months (2024--2025). The split is never
shuffled, so the test period lies strictly in the future of every training
sample, which is the temporal analogue of avoiding data leakage
(Section~\ref{sec:bg_trading}).

The validation year exists to carry decisions. The saturation study that
fixes where the state ladder stops, and the choice of the penalty weight
$\lambda$, are both settled on 2023 and never on the test years. Under the
two-way split used earlier in the project there was no way to make that
separation, and therefore no way to demonstrate that a design choice had
not been influenced by the months used to report the final numbers.
Episode arrays and metadata are cached as \texttt{.npz} and \texttt{.json},
so every experiment is reproducible offline and no run can silently train
on differently-prepared data.

\begin{figure}[h]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.95\textwidth]{chrono_split}
  \caption{Chronological three-way split of the 96 monthly episodes:
  60 training months, 12 validation months and 24 held-out test months.}
  \label{fig:split}
\end{figure}

The eight-year window is not homogeneous, which is deliberate. Training
data includes the 2018 volatility spikes, the 2020 COVID crash and
rebound, and the 2022 bear market; validation covers the 2023 recovery;
and the held-out test window covers 2024 and 2025. An agent trained on
this window has met falling markets, so it cannot excuse its test
behaviour by never having seen one.

\section{The trading environment}
\label{sec:env_impl}

\texttt{TradingEnv} subclasses \texttt{gym.Env} with
\texttt{action\_space} $=$ \texttt{Discrete(3)}. Internally it tracks cash
$C_t$, fractional units $h_t$, the cost basis of the open position
(average-cost accounting) and the running peak of wealth, from which the
account features are computed on demand.

\paragraph{The environment receives features rather than computing them.}
The constructor takes a precomputed market matrix aligned to its price
series and validates its width against the feature registry. This is a
structural guarantee rather than a convenience: an environment that never
computes a rolling window cannot truncate one at an episode boundary. The
defect described in Section~\ref{sec:defects} is not fixed here so much as
made unrepresentable.

\paragraph{The observed feature set is selectable.} The constructor takes
a list of feature names and emits observations in the canonical order of
\texttt{features.py} filtered by that list, so
\texttt{observation\_space} is $\mathbb{R}^9$ for the state of
Table~\ref{tab:state} and $\mathbb{R}^{10}$ for the risk-aware extension.
Every rung of the ladder therefore runs through one code path, which means a
measured difference between two rungs cannot be an implementation
difference between two environments.

\paragraph{Admissibility is enforced at construction.} If
\texttt{risk\_lambda} is positive and \texttt{drawdown} is not among the
requested features, the constructor raises. The argument of
Section~\ref{sec:risk_extension} is therefore checked by the program rather
than asserted in prose: the inadmissible pairing of $S_3$ with a
risk-aware reward cannot be instantiated, whether by a script, by a
command-line argument or by mistake. \texttt{run\_final.py} attempts the
construction deliberately and records that it was refused, so the guard is
evidenced in the results file.

\paragraph{Trade execution and the action model.} Under the default slice
model a Buy spends $\min(C_t,\, \Delta)$ dollars with
$\Delta = 0.25\,C_0$, acquiring $x/(P_t(1+c))$ units, and a Sell liquidates
$\min(h_t P_t,\, \Delta)$ of position value, crediting $u P_t (1-c)$. Under
the whole-share model a Buy spends one share price and a Sell releases one
unit. Both are implemented, selected by a constructor argument, because
Section~\ref{sec:actions} treats action representation as an experimental
axis in its own right. Residual position value below the \$1 minimum trade
size is flushed to cash, so that ``flat'' means flat and dust cannot
accumulate outside the mask logic.

\paragraph{Masking and safety.} \texttt{action\_masks()} returns the legal
action set each step. If a caller nevertheless submits an illegal action,
the environment executes Hold instead and flags the event in \texttt{info};
the harness accumulates this as the illegal-action diagnostic.

\paragraph{Reward and its bookkeeping.} \texttt{info} carries the pure
wealth change \texttt{dw} and the risk penalty separately from the returned
reward. This separation is what allows the accounting identity to be
checked under a shaped reward, and it lets a risk-trained agent be
\emph{evaluated} on wealth change so that it is compared with the other
agents on the same quantity it will ultimately be judged by.

\paragraph{Termination.} At the final bar the environment force-liquidates
any open position at the closing price with the fee applied, implementing
the mandatory end-of-episode liquidation of Section~\ref{sec:actions}.

\section{From-scratch REINFORCE}
\label{sec:impl_reinforce}

The policy network is a two-hidden-layer MLP
($9 \to 128 \to 128 \to 3$, $\tanh$ activations). Sampling uses a masked
categorical distribution: illegal logits are set to $-10^9$ before the
softmax, so probability mass is renormalised over legal actions only ---
the network is never trained on, and can never propose, an illegal trade.

One gradient update is performed per episode. The core of the training
loop is four lines, in exact correspondence with the derivation of
Section~\ref{sec:pg_derivation}:

\begin{verbatim}
# rewards-to-go, standardised within the episode
G[t] = r[t] + gamma * G[t+1]
G = (G - G.mean()) / (G.std() + 1e-8)
loss = -(log_probs * G).sum()      # L(theta) = -sum_t log pi(a|s) * G_t
loss.backward(); optimizer.step()  # gradient ascent on J
\end{verbatim}

The returns tensor carries no gradient (it is data, not a function of
$\theta$ in the surrogate loss), matching Step~6 of the derivation.

\section{From-scratch Deep Q-learning}
\label{sec:impl_dqn}

The Q-network is an MLP of the same capacity
($9 \to 128 \to 128 \to 3$, ReLU). The implementation contains the three
standard DQN components, each present for a reason stated in
Section~\ref{sec:dqn}:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Replay buffer} (50k transitions, uniform sampling,
        minibatch 64): breaks the temporal correlation of consecutive
        market steps.
  \item \textbf{Target network} (synchronised every 500 steps): stabilises
        the bootstrapped TD target.
  \item \textbf{Masked $\varepsilon$-greedy} ($\varepsilon: 1.0 \to 0.05$
        over the first half of training): exploration restricted to legal
        actions; the $\max$ in the TD target is likewise taken over the
        \emph{stored} legal-action mask of the next state.
\end{itemize}

Storing the next-state mask in the buffer is a subtle but necessary
detail: without it, the target could bootstrap from an action that is
illegal in $s_{t+1}$ (e.g.\ Sell while flat), silently overestimating value.

The core update, in exact correspondence with
Equation~(\ref{eq:dqn_loss}):

\begin{verbatim}
q_next = target_net(s_next)               # Q_phi-minus(s', .)
q_next[~mask_next] = -1e9                 # max over LEGAL actions only
y = r + gamma * (1 - done) * q_next.max(dim=1).values
loss = mse(qnet(s).gather(1, a), y.detach())   # TD loss on Bellman targets
loss.backward(); optimizer.step()
\end{verbatim}

\noindent The \texttt{y.detach()} matters: the target is data, not a
function to differentiate through --- gradients flow only into
$Q_\phi(s,a)$, exactly as the semi-gradient TD rule prescribes
\cite{sutton2018}.

\section{PPO ablation row}
\label{sec:impl_ppo}

The third method, PPO, uses a reference implementation rather than a
from-scratch one, because its role is to answer whether a modern stabilised
policy-gradient method behaves differently on this problem, and a
hand-written approximation of PPO would confound that question with the
quality of the reimplementation.

\paragraph{Masking, and why the library choice matters.} Plain PPO from
Stable-Baselines3 \cite{raffin2021sb3} has no native action masking. Using
it would leave the policy free to place probability mass on infeasible
trades and rely on the environment's forced-Hold override to absorb them,
which is a materially different learning problem from the one REINFORCE and
Deep Q-learning solve: their distributions are renormalised over legal
actions before any action is sampled. Comparing a masked learner with an
unmasked one and attributing the difference to the algorithm would be
unsound. PPO is therefore taken from \texttt{sb3-contrib} in its
\texttt{MaskablePPO} form \cite{raffin2021sb3}, which applies the same
feasibility constraint through the same \texttt{action\_masks()} call, so
all three methods face an identical action space at every step.

\paragraph{Matching the episode distribution and the seed.} SB3 treats an
environment as one long-running task, whereas the trading problem is a
distribution over months. A thin wrapper (\texttt{MonthSampler}) draws a
new training month on every \texttt{reset()} from a generator seeded by the
run seed. The seeding is the point. In the earlier implementation this
sampler was seeded to a constant, so changing the run seed altered only the
network initialisation and left the sequence of months identical; PPO's
reported seed-to-seed spread then measured less variation than the other two
methods' did, and the three were not comparable on stability. The wrapper
now takes the run seed, so every method's spread reflects the same two
sources of variation: initialisation and the order in which months are seen.

\section{What verification found}
\label{sec:defects}

Verification is reported here as a result rather than a formality, because
it changed the experiment. An audit of the earlier implementation, carried
out before the final runs, found twelve defects, of which three were severe
enough to invalidate the numbers the earlier version produced. Two were
quantified directly on the real data by a dedicated script.

\paragraph{Rolling features were truncated at episode boundaries.} The
earlier environment computed momentum and volatility inside each episode
from that episode's prices alone. Every month therefore opened with a
momentum of exactly zero and a volatility of exactly zero, values that were
not measurements but artefacts of where the calendar had been cut. On the
real series, 43\% of each episode's steps carried a window that was
partially or wholly unfilled. The agent was being trained to open positions
on the basis of a feature that was, at that moment, guaranteed to be blank.

\paragraph{The buy-and-hold benchmark was not buy-and-hold.} The earlier
baseline bought one slice whenever a Buy was legal, inside an environment
whose slice was a tenth of capital. Such a policy needs ten steps to become
fully invested and so averages roughly 0.69 exposure across a month rather
than 1.0. It is a ten-day dollar-cost average wearing the name of
buy-and-hold, and because the asset drifts upward it is systematically
weaker than the strategy it was labelled as. Measured on the test months,
true buy-and-hold earns 21.6\% more than the policy previously reported
under that name. Every agent compared against it was flattered by the
difference. Both are now reported, under names that say what they are, and
buy-and-hold is pinned to the slice action model so that it means the same
thing even in configurations where the agent trades whole shares.

\paragraph{The risk-aware reward was not measurable from the state.} The
earlier experiment penalised drawdown while observing a state that did not
contain it, which is the violation Section~\ref{sec:state_admissible}
describes. The result was not an MDP, so the derivations of Chapter~3 did
not apply to it and its outcome could not be interpreted. This is now
prevented by construction rather than corrected by convention.

The remaining nine defects were smaller but pointed the same way: closing
prices retained while the rest of the OHLCV record was discarded; a two-way
split with no validation set; data ending a year earlier than available; the
PPO seeding and masking asymmetries described above; drawdown measured only
across months and never within one; a feature that carried no information;
and an accounting check applied to the shaped reward, where it must fail for
a correct environment, rather than to the wealth change. Each is recorded
with its fix, and the audit is what motivated rebuilding the experiment
package rather than patching it.

The general lesson is worth stating, because it is the one an examiner is
entitled to ask about. None of these defects announced itself. Every one
produced plausible numbers, and the two quantified above both biased results
in the flattering direction. They were found by asking what each component
was supposed to guarantee and then testing that specific claim, which is
why the following section describes the tests in terms of the properties
they defend rather than the functions they call.

\section{Verification and testing}
\label{sec:testing}

Two layers of verification precede any market experiment.

\paragraph{Unit tests.} Twenty-one tests (\texttt{test\_v2.py}) are
organised around the properties they defend rather than the functions they
exercise. All pass.

\emph{Feature contracts.} No feature may use data from after the bar it is
reported for; the test truncates the series at $t$, recomputes, and requires
the values at $t$ to be unchanged. Rolling features must be continuous
across a month boundary; the test computes them on a continuous series and
on the concatenation of its monthly pieces and requires the first to differ
from the second, which is the presence of the defect being asserted as
absent from the pipeline.

\emph{MDP contracts.} The accounting identity
$\sum_t \Delta W_t = W_T - W_0$ must hold under random legal play, checked
against the wealth change rather than the reward. With $\lambda = 0$ the
reward must equal the wealth change exactly. With $\lambda > 0$ and
\texttt{drawdown} absent, construction must raise.

\emph{Feature relationships.} The episode's running profit must be exactly
recoverable from cash and exposure, which is the arithmetic that justifies
excluding it. Drawdown must \emph{not} be recoverable: the test exhibits two
states that agree on every other feature and differ in drawdown, so its
inclusion is shown to be necessary rather than argued to be.

\emph{Behavioural contracts.} Sell is masked while flat and Buy once cash is
exhausted; the illegal-action override engages and is reported; forced
liquidation leaves zero units; the clock runs from 0 to 1; the two action
models differ in the direction claimed; each reference strategy attains the
exposure its description promises; and the data pipeline's three splits are
non-empty, disjoint and chronologically ordered.

\paragraph{A diagnostic for whether the state is used at all.} One check
deserves separate mention, because it was added in response to the first
set of results rather than written in advance, and because it changed how
those results are read. \texttt{mask\_determined} records, for every pattern
of legal actions the policy encounters, which actions it chose while that
pattern held. If each pattern maps to exactly one action, the policy is a
function of feasibility alone and its observation is unused.

The motivation was an anomaly rather than a suspicion. Across ladder rungs
from one feature to twelve, several trained agents returned wealth changes
that were not merely similar but bit-for-bit identical to a reference
strategy's, which is not something two policies reading different
observations would do. The diagnostic confirmed the explanation, and it is
reported for every learned row in Chapter~5 because a method that ignores
its state cannot inform a comparison between states.

\paragraph{Algorithm validation on known-outcome tasks (Phase 0).} Before
touching market data, the same algorithm implementations were validated on
games where success is unambiguous: CartPole-v1, Flappy Bird and
LunarLander-v3. Every learner clears its random floor (e.g.\ CartPole:
random 28.8 vs.\ REINFORCE 292 and DQN 500/500). A learning algorithm that
cannot solve a game with a known answer cannot be trusted on markets,
where the correct answer is unknown --- this ordering was a fixed
methodological requirement of the project.

\section{Reproducibility}
\label{sec:repro}

Every claim in Chapter~5 is regenerable from committed code and cached
data. Specifically: (i) all random number generators, in Python, NumPy and
PyTorch, are seeded, and results are reported over all six seeds rather than
a favourable one; (ii) the data pipeline caches its output, so experiments
never silently retrain on differently-prepared data; (iii) each experiment
writes a JSON record containing the configuration, per-month results,
training traces, diagnostics and provenance, the last including the git
commit and whether the working tree was clean when the run started, so a
result can always be traced to the exact code that produced it; and (iv) the
whole suite runs on CPU in about an hour, with no result depending on
specialised hardware. That last property is deliberate: every number in this
dissertation can be regenerated from scratch during a viva demonstration.

Two selection decisions are also recorded in the results files rather than
described only in prose. The ladder's stopping rule and the rule for
choosing $\lambda$ are both evaluated in code, each carries a flag
recording that it was declared before the runs, and each stores the
comparisons it made. A reader can therefore check that the stopping point
follows from the stated criterion instead of taking that on trust.

\paragraph{Chapter summary (plain language).} The mathematics of Chapter~3
now exists as a handful of small, separately testable programs: one that
fetches and organises eight years of real market data; one that simulates
the trading month, charging fees and enforcing the rules; two learners
built from the ground up, each of whose central lines of code is a direct
transcription of a derived equation; and a harness that runs every method
and every benchmark through exactly the same evaluation path.

Two design decisions in that code are doing more work than they appear to.
The simulator is handed its market indicators rather than computing them,
which makes it structurally incapable of the boundary defect described
above. And it refuses to be built at all if the score it is asked to use
depends on something the agent cannot see, so the consistency argument of
Chapter~3 is enforced by the program instead of promised in prose.

Before being allowed near market data, both learners had to prove
themselves on video games with known answers, and the simulator had to pass
twenty-one tests, including that its per-step wealth changes always sum
exactly to the month's true profit. Those checks were not a formality: they
turned up twelve real defects in the earlier version of this work, two of
which had been quietly making the results look better than they were. The
next chapter reports what happened once they were fixed.
