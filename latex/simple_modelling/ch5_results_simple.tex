\chapter{Empirical Results \& Analysis}
\label{ch:results_simple}

This chapter reports every experiment run to date on the simple-modelling track, in the order the methodology builds: Phase~0 validates the learning algorithms on games with known outcomes; Phase~1 applies the validated PPO loop to real SPY data under Iteration~1 (3-D state) and Iteration~2 (5-D state). All numbers below are produced by committed scripts and are reproducible from the repository; none are illustrative placeholders.

\section{Experimental setup and evaluation metrics}
\label{sec:setup_simple}

\paragraph{Phase 0 (games).} Each algorithm trains under a fixed seed (42) with a fixed interaction budget (50{,}000 environment timesteps on CartPole; 150{,}000 on Flappy Bird and LunarLander; REINFORCE uses an equivalent episode budget), then is evaluated greedily over 30 fresh episodes. The metric is mean evaluation return.

\paragraph{Phase 1 (trading).} SPY daily closes 2018--2024, split chronologically into 58 training months and 26 held-out test months (Nov 2022 -- Dec 2024). Starting cash $C_0 = \$10{,}000$, transaction cost $c = 5$ basis points, PPO budget 80{,}000 timesteps, seed 42. Metrics per test month: wealth change $\Delta W$ (dollars), trades per month, and the win rate against the strongest passive baseline. Two diagnostics accompany every run: the accounting identity (rewards must sum to the wealth change; tolerance $10^{-6}$) and the illegal-action request rate.

Three observations carry forward. First, every implementation clears its random floor on every game, which is the correctness evidence Phase~0 exists to provide. Second, the from-scratch REINFORCE learns on all three games but with visibly higher variance and a lower plateau --- the textbook motivation for actor--critic and clipped updates. Third, tabular Q-learning reaches 500 on CartPole \emph{only after} the continuous state is hand-binned into a $6{\times}6{\times}12{\times}12$ table; no such binning is available for a trading state with continuous cash and prices, which is why the trading experiments use policy-gradient methods with function approximation. On the harder games, PPO is the most consistent learner (best on Flappy Bird; within noise of DQN on LunarLander), supporting its selection for Phase~1.

\section{Phase 1, Iteration 1: the 3-D state agent}
\label{sec:phase1_iter1_results}

Table~\ref{tab:phase1_iter1} compares PPO on \texttt{TradingEnv-v0} against four baselines with explicit objectives: B0 never trades; B1 buys a single share on the first bar and holds; B1b invests all available cash on the first bars and holds (the fair passive benchmark when $C_0 \gg P_t$); B2 selects uniformly among feasible actions.

\centering
\caption{Iteration 1 on the 26 held-out SPY test months. $\Delta W$ in dollars; fee 5\,bps; PPO budget 80k steps.}
\label{tab:phase1_iter1}
\begin{tabular}{lrrrr}
\toprule
\textbf{Method} & \textbf{Mean $\Delta W$} & \textbf{Std} & \textbf{Trades/mo} & \textbf{Win vs B1b} \\
\midrule
B0 do-nothing        & 0.00   & 0.00   & 0.0  & 31\% \\
B1 buy-one-hold      & 7.97   & 16.67  & 1.0  & 31\% \\
B1b buy-max-hold     & 76.91  & 178.68 & 19.1 & --- \\
B2 random (masked)   & 14.14  & 32.93  & 12.8 & 31\% \\
A1 PPO (3-D state)   & 76.91  & 178.68 & 19.1 & 0\% \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

\paragraph{The honest headline.} The trained PPO policy produced an action sequence \emph{identical to B1b on all 26 test months}: under the pure wealth-change reward with a 3-D state, the optimal learned behaviour is simply ``get fully invested and stay there.'' The agent's apparent +\$76.91 is therefore not evidence of skill beyond buy-and-hold --- and this equivalence would have been invisible had the comparison stopped at the one-share baseline B1, which PPO ``beats'' by construction. The accounting identity held exactly on every episode, and the deterministic policy requested an infeasible action on 3.9\% of steps (executed as Hold), indicating the constraints are mostly, but not perfectly, internalised.

The richer state \emph{did} break the buy-and-hold collapse --- the Iteration-2 policy matches B1b on zero months --- but it converged to the opposite corner: a cautious, mostly-flat policy. Inspection of the policy's action distribution over a representative test month shows a mean Hold probability of 0.66 (Buy 0.12, Sell 0.22), so the deterministic (argmax) evaluation never trades; sampling actions stochastically yields a small positive mean $\Delta W$ of +\$1.87. On month-by-month wealth the flat agent still beats the fully-invested benchmark in 8 of 26 months --- the months when SPY fell.

\section{Analysis and key findings}
\label{sec:analysis_simple}

\paragraph{1. The reward, not the state, sets the incentive.} Under $r_t = \Delta W_t$ in a mostly-rising test window, full investment is near-optimal, and the 3-D agent found exactly that. Adding entry and time context (Iteration 2) did not create trading skill; it moved the policy along the exposure axis, from always-invested to almost-never-invested. Both extremes are rational responses to a risk-neutral reward estimated from noisy monthly episodes --- the training window (2018--2022) contains the COVID crash and the 2022 bear market, where staying flat avoids large negative rewards.

\paragraph{2. Baseline design is a result in itself.} The single most informative row in this chapter is B1b. Without the fair fully-invested benchmark, Iteration 1 would have been reported as ``PPO beats buy-and-hold by 10$\times$'' (against B1); with it, the correct statement is ``PPO rediscovered buy-and-hold.''

\paragraph{3. What Iteration 3 must change.} Because the state upgrade alone shifted exposure rather than timing, the next iteration targets the objective: a reward of the form $r_t = \Delta W_t - \lambda D_t$ (penalising drawdown $D_t$) gives the agent a reason to hold \emph{selectively} --- invested in calm regimes, flat in falling ones --- which neither extreme policy can satisfy. The masked-action rate (3.9\% in Iteration 1) and the accounting identity (exact in every run) carry forward unchanged as standing diagnostics.

\paragraph{Scope.} These are single-seed results at a fixed budget, on monthly episodes of daily bars. Multi-seed confidence intervals, minute-bar episodes ($T{=}390$), and the drawdown-penalised reward are the planned extensions, in that order.
