\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}
An investor holding a stock-index fund faces the same decision each day:
buy more, sell part of the position, or do nothing. This dissertation
asks whether reinforcement learning, which learns by trial and error
from rewards, can make these decisions more effectively than the simple
strategy of buying and holding permanently.

Three algorithms are compared: REINFORCE and Deep Q-learning,
implemented from scratch, and Proximal Policy Optimisation, from an
open-source library. Each agent trades the S\&P 500 exchange-traded fund
(SPY) in monthly episodes with transaction fees, action masking, and a
trade size calibrated on validation data. Every configuration is trained
with six random seeds, and every experimental decision is made on
validation data before the test data is touched.

The work consists of two studies. The first trained the agents on real
daily data alone: 60 monthly episodes from 2018--2022, tested on
2024--2025. No agent beat buy-and-hold, which earned +\$180.25 per
month. A behavioural analysis showed why the returns could not be
trusted: the two policy-gradient methods earned their returns from fixed
always-buy patterns that never responded to market information, on
twelve seeds out of twelve, while the only method whose actions did
depend on its state, Deep Q-learning, earned the least. The diagnosis is
a data bottleneck. Sixty mostly-rising episodes cannot train networks of
18,000 parameters beyond memorisation.

The main study removed that constraint. A market simulator with three
controlled conditions --- rising, falling, and flat --- was calibrated
on the same training years and generated 3,000 balanced episodes, and
the networks were reduced to 2,800 parameters. Under these conditions the ranking
inverted. Deep Q-learning earned +\$126.94 per episode, on all six
seeds, on a balanced test set where no fixed trading pattern is
profitable, and kept its edge at ten times the assumed transaction fee.
The risk-aware reward, which had only suppressed trading in the first
study, now cut falling-market losses by 73\% at a 1.5\% cost in mean
return. Transferred unchanged to 180 real months that no training or
tuning decision ever saw (2006--2017 and 2023--2025), the learned
policies remained profitable on every seed, held zero exposure through
October 2008, and earned a higher Sharpe ratio than buy-and-hold (0.224
against 0.198).

The dissertation contributes a verified evaluation framework, a
behavioural test of whether a policy uses its state, a calibrated market
simulator for controlled evaluation in each market condition, and evidence that the
amount and balance of training data --- not the choice of algorithm ---
set the limit on what reinforcement learning can learn in this setting.
