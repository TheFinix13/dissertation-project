\section{Motivation}
\label{sec:motivation}

This chapter explains the problem in plain language, states what this dissertation claims, and maps the rest of the document. The dissertation does \emph{not} invent a new learning algorithm. It tests one design idea: feed the agent a \textbf{confidence signal} from its own forecaster, and use that signal to trade smaller or stop buying when the model is unsure.

\paragraph{The institutional reality.} Many investment mandates cap \emph{drawdown}: the percentage fall from the portfolio's running peak, not from the starting balance. CalPERS, Yale's endowment, and Bridgewater's All Weather fund all report performance in terms that treat peak-to-trough loss as a first-class constraint~\parencite{calpers2022policy,yale2023endowment,dalio2017principles}. In every case the mandate has the same shape: stay above a floor relative to peak, and still beat cash.

\paragraph{The behavioural angle.} Retail investors feel losses about twice as strongly as equivalent gains~\parencite{tversky1992cumulative}, so drawdown --- not daily volatility --- is what triggers panic selling.

\paragraph{Why the standard toolkit struggles.} Mean-variance optimisation~\parencite{markowitz1952portfolio} is a one-shot weight choice: it cannot react when the regime changes mid-year and it has no running peak. Value-at-Risk (VaR) and expected shortfall~\parencite{rockafellar2000optimization} describe a bad day, not a long path of losses from peak. The Sortino ratio~\parencite{sortino1994downside} still works period-by-period. Drawdown-aware static programmes~\parencite{magdon2004sample,chekhlov2005drawdown} use the right objective but pick one allocation and hold it.

\paragraph{Where machine learning enters.} \emph{Reinforcement learning} trains an agent that asks ``what should I do today given what I have seen so far?'' by simulating many trading days. Prior finance work~\parencite{jiang2017deep,yang2020deep,liu2021finrl} applied this template to portfolio choice but usually rewarded return only and fed the policy a single point forecast with no measure of forecast confidence.

\paragraph{Two gaps this dissertation closes.} First, the literature rarely bakes drawdown-style caution into the action itself; risk enters through the reward, not through how large each trade is allowed to be. Second, the policy never sees whether the forecaster is sure or guessing. This work adds a confidence signal in two places: as extra context in the agent's state, and as a guard that blocks new buys when confidence is low. The result is compared honestly to static optimisers, manual stop rules, and a confidence-blind PPO baseline.

\section{Problem statement}
\label{sec:problem_statement}

Practice today sits in three camps, and this dissertation is measured against each.

\paragraph{Static optimisers} (mean-variance, risk parity, Conditional Drawdown-at-Risk) choose weights once. They do not adapt when the market regime flips mid-window.

\paragraph{Reactive rules} (trailing stop-losses with moving-average re-entry) are common in discretionary trading. The stop fires after the loss; re-entry often misses the rebound. Chapter~\ref{ch:results} shows 5\,\% and 10\,\% trailing stops on 2022--2025 that finish below passive buy-and-hold because they sell into the dip and sit in cash through the recovery.

\paragraph{Learning-based traders} in the literature~\parencite{jiang2017deep,yang2020deep,liu2021finrl} optimise return on point forecasts with no confidence channel and no native drawdown guard in the trade size itself.

The candidate tested here is a PPO agent --- a standard reinforcement-learning policy --- that reads a confidence signal from a probabilistic LSTM forecaster. The signal enters twice: as context in the state, and as a hard rule that blocks new long-side buys when confidence crosses a threshold set on validation data.

Formally, the agent should maximise risk-adjusted return while keeping terminal value above 95\,\% of the running peak (the capital-preservation floor used in the headline experiment), with bounded trade size and a small per-trade cost. Section~\ref{sec:problem_formulation} writes this objective in the language of Markov decision processes.

\paragraph{Bounded scope.} The headline test window is 1 January 2022 to 31 December 2025. This dissertation makes no claims about behaviour outside that window. The instruments evaluated are US-listed single-name equities and exchange-traded funds (ETFs); generalisation to fixed income, foreign exchange, or non-US equity markets is not tested. The candidate is not claimed to dominate every alternative on every regime; Chapter~\ref{ch:discussion} names two regimes (sustained low-uncertainty bull markets and very-low-drawdown defensive holdings) in which the uncertainty-aware agent under-performs and explains why.

\section{Aims and objectives}
\label{sec:objectives}

The dissertation pursues five objectives, listed in the order in which they are answered in the rest of the document.

\paragraph{O1 --- the core scientific question.} Does a reinforcement-learning agent that reads its own forecaster's \textbf{confidence signal} deliver better drawdown-aware, risk-adjusted returns than agents that ignore that signal? Operationally: a probabilistic LSTM~\parencite{salinas2020deepar} outputs a mean and a spread each day; a PPO policy~\parencite{schulman2017ppo} uses the spread both as state context and as a buy guard when confidence is low.

\paragraph{O2 --- the headline empirical comparison.} On SPY (a liquid ETF tracking the US large-cap index) over 2022--2025, compare the confidence-aware agent to buy-and-hold, a 5\,\% trailing stop, and a confidence-blind PPO baseline. Report Sharpe ratio, final wealth, drawdown from peak, preservation ratio, and how often daily returns breach a 95\,\% Value-at-Risk threshold.

\paragraph{O3 --- generalisation.} Repeat on 70 US stocks and ETFs, and on four sliding two-year test windows (2018--2025) so hyper-parameters are never tuned on the test data being reported.

\paragraph{O4 --- reproducibility.} Fix splits, seeds, scripts, and metrics so an external reader can reproduce every table from the public repository (Appendix~\ref{app:reproducibility}).

\paragraph{O5 --- honest wins and losses.} Name the market conditions where the method helps (volatile recoveries, high-volatility single names) and where it hurts (slow bull markets, already-calm defensives), and state when a confidence signal is worth the complexity and when it is not (Chapter~\ref{ch:discussion}).

Objectives O1 and O2 are answered in Chapter~\ref{ch:results}. O3 is answered in Sections~\ref{sec:phase2_full_grid} and~\ref{sec:walk_forward}. O4 is answered in Section~\ref{sec:evaluation_protocol} and Appendix~\ref{app:reproducibility}. O5 is answered in Chapter~\ref{ch:discussion}.

\section{Contributions}
\label{sec:contributions}

The contributions are deliberately modest: PPO, probabilistic forecasting, and uncertainty-aware trading already exist separately. This dissertation adds a controlled test of one specific wiring --- confidence signal into trade size and buy guard --- on a reproducible four-way comparison.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item A trading environment where each trade is shrunk by $(1 - u_t)$ (the confidence signal) with a floor $s_{\min}$, and new buys are blocked when $u_t$ exceeds threshold $\tau$ (Section~\ref{sec:env_and_contribution}). Delivers O1.
  \item A like-for-like comparison on SPY against buy-and-hold, a trailing stop, and confidence-blind PPO (Chapter~\ref{ch:results}). Delivers O2.
  \item Extension to 70 stocks and four walk-forward time windows (Sections~\ref{sec:phase2_full_grid},~\ref{sec:walk_forward}). Delivers O3.
  \item Fixed protocol, public code, and runnable notebooks (Section~\ref{sec:evaluation_protocol}, Appendix~\ref{app:reproducibility}). Delivers O4.
  \item Discussion of when drawdown numbers mislead, which metric pair matches the mandate, and which regimes the agent wins or loses (Chapter~\ref{ch:discussion}). Delivers O5.
  \item A second confidence-signal source (epistemic via Monte-Carlo dropout~\parencite{gal2016dropout}) tested honestly against the headline aleatoric signal; the two are close on wealth, with a small Sharpe edge to aleatoric (Section~\ref{sec:phase2_inference}).
  \item Paired statistical tests (Wilcoxon, bootstrap intervals) on the 70-stock grid so headline gaps are not dismissed as luck (\texttt{experiments/stats\_significance.py}).
  \item A calibration check showing the forecaster's 80\,\% intervals cover 81\,\% of validation days --- the threshold the policy actually uses (Section~\ref{sec:forecaster_calibration}).
\end{enumerate}
