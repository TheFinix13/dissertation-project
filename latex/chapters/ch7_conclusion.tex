\chapter{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{ch:conclusion}

\section{Summary}
\label{sec:summary}

This dissertation asked a simple question: if a trading agent can see how confident its own forecaster is, does it manage drawdown-aware growth better than agents that cannot? On the Phase-2 evidence, the answer is yes --- the gain is large, repeats across stocks and time windows, and survives formal statistical checks.

\paragraph{What was built.} A PPO~\parencite{schulman2017ppo} trading agent paired with a probabilistic LSTM forecaster~\parencite{hochreiter1997lstm,salinas2020deepar}. Each day the forecaster outputs a mean return and a spread; the spread becomes a \textbf{confidence signal} $u_t$. The signal enters twice: the agent sees it as context, and a guard blocks new buys when confidence is too low. The only new mathematics is Equation~\ref{eq:probabilistic_trade_size}, which shrinks trade size when $u_t$ is high. Two signal sources were tested: market noise (aleatoric) and model unfamiliarity (epistemic via Monte-Carlo dropout~\parencite{gal2016dropout}).

\paragraph{What was found --- Phase-2 headline.} The full Phase-2 grid trained each agent variant on 70 stocks with 10 random seeds and 50,000 PPO time-steps per cell, producing 2,097 completed cells across three experimental arms. The results are unambiguous:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item The probabilistic agents (both aleatoric and epistemic) grew a \$1\,M starting portfolio to a \emph{median} of \$1.6\,M across the 70-stock universe on the 2022--2025 test window, with a median Sharpe ratio above $+0.68$ and a win-rate (cells finishing above \$1\,M) of 89--92\,\%. The gap against the baseline survives paired statistical scrutiny: a paired Wilcoxon signed-rank on the 70 matched tickers rejects the null at $p < 10^{-12}$ on terminal value, Sharpe and maximum drawdown, with a 95\,\% paired-bootstrap interval for the terminal-value advantage of \$559k to \$814k (Table~\ref{tab:phase2_stats}).
  \item The baseline PPO, without the uncertainty signal, finished at a median of \$999\,063 --- essentially flat --- with a median Sharpe of $-0.04$ and a win-rate of 46\,\%. The baseline agent learned to do almost nothing, because without a confidence signal it could not distinguish dangerous days from safe ones.
  \item The two uncertainty modes (aleatoric and epistemic) produced close headline numbers, with a small but detectable Sharpe edge to aleatoric. The terminal-value gap between the two arms is not significant at the 5\,\% level ($p = 0.080$); the Sharpe gap is detectable at the sample size ($+0.014$ in favour of aleatoric, $p = 7 \times 10^{-4}$). The honest reading is that any calibrated uncertainty signal helps, and that the source of the signal is a small second-order effect compared to the gap from either arm to the baseline.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Walk-forward confirmation.} The walk-forward validation tested the agent on four independent two-year windows covering 2018--2025 (including the COVID crash and the 2022 inflation shock). Across 320 walk-forward cells, the probabilistic agent achieved a median final value of \$1,167,050, a median Sharpe of $+0.55$, and a win-rate of 87.2\,\% --- compared to the baseline's \$997,591, $-0.10$, and 46.9\,\%. The advantage held on every single fold. This is strong evidence that the agent learned a general strategy, not a pattern specific to one test period.

\paragraph{What was tested but did not change the headline.} The epistemic arm (Arm~C) was added in response to project-proposal feedback. At full Phase-2 scale, its median final value (\$1,619,713) and win-rate (92.3\,\%) are close to the aleatoric arm's (\$1,612,478, 89.1\,\%) but the paired statistics in Table~\ref{tab:phase2_stats} record a detectable Sharpe and drawdown edge to aleatoric at this sample size. The dissertation reports both arms honestly. The practical implication is that a practitioner can use either uncertainty source and recover most of the headline gain against the baseline; the small advantage to aleatoric is a refinement, not an architectural decision.

\paragraph{What the ablation refined.} Section~\ref{sec:ablation} reports a three-arm ablation of Equation~\ref{eq:probabilistic_trade_size} on SPY at the canonical Phase-2 budget. Each single piece of the design recovers about 95\,\% of the terminal-value gain over the baseline, but raises median maximum drawdown from $18.5$\,\% to $21$--$22$\,\% --- a penalty significant at $p = 2 \times 10^{-3}$ on every contrast. The contribution therefore separates cleanly: any calibrated uncertainty signal in the loop delivers the return; the \emph{full} two-piece design delivers the drawdown safety that the stated objective requires.

\paragraph{What is not claimed.} The dissertation does not propose a new reinforcement-learning algorithm; PPO is unchanged. It does not propose a new forecasting architecture; the probabilistic LSTM is a 2020 idea built on a 1997 idea. It does not claim multi-asset portfolio optimisation; one agent runs per ticker. It does not claim live-trading performance; all evidence is backtest evidence on adjusted-close daily data. It does not claim the design works in every regime; Section~\ref{sec:wins_and_losses} names regimes (sustained low-uncertainty bull markets and very-low-drawdown defensives) in which it under-performs and explains why. Six out of 2,100 cells failed (0.29\,\%), all on a single small-cap biotech ticker (RYTM), due to a numerical edge case in the Gaussian loss.

\section{Future work}
\label{sec:future_work}

The future-work items are split into three groups. Group~1 lists optional extensions that could be completed before the September 2026 submission or in response to examiner feedback; the SPY two-piece ablation (Section~\ref{sec:ablation}) is already reported in the body and is not repeated here. Group~2 is a real-world deployment roadmap, explicitly out of scope. Group~3 is post-dissertation research.

\subsection{Group 1 --- Optional extensions before submission}
\label{sec:group_1_scheduled}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{Sector-aware uncertainty calibration.} Replace the single global uncertainty-guard threshold ($\tau = 0.80$) with per-sector or per-regime thresholds calibrated on the validation window. Section~\ref{sec:wins_and_losses} identifies persistent-trend single names as the regime where the global threshold costs the most; this is the most surgical fix.

  \item \textbf{Extend the ablation to the full 70-ticker grid.} The SPY pilot in Section~\ref{sec:ablation} already isolates the state-only, guard-only and scaling-only arms at the canonical Phase-2 budget. Re-running the same three arms on \texttt{market\_sample} (about 2--3 hours per arm on a Colab T4) would test whether the drawdown-control finding generalises beyond a single index ETF; the runner and commands are in Appendix~\ref{app:ablation}.

  \item \textbf{Sensitivity sweep.} Sweep the uncertainty quantile threshold over $\{0.7, 0.8, 0.9\}$, the minimum scale $s_{\min}$ over $\{0.05, 0.10, 0.20\}$, and the maximum trade fraction $f_{\max}$ over $\{0.05, 0.10, 0.20\}$. Report the headline metrics over the resulting 27-cell grid on SPY.

  \item \textbf{Bootstrap-augmented training.} Generate synthetic training paths by block-bootstrap resampling of historical return sequences~\parencite{politis1994stationary} to expand the effective training set without leaving the empirical return distribution.

  \item \textbf{Shock-window case studies.} Score the agents on the protocol shock periods (COVID 2020, Ukraine-war onset 2022) as standalone case studies, with per-window equity curves and metric tables.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Group 2 --- Live deployment (out of scope of this dissertation)}
\label{sec:group_2_deployment}

A separate engineering question is whether the trained agent can be made useful against a real, live portfolio. The dissertation does not address that question: all evidence reported here is backtest evidence on adjusted-close daily data and the candidate is not claimed to be ready for execution against real capital without further validation. Any live-deployment work --- shadow paper-trading, decision-support advisory, scheduled execution with pre-trade risk gates --- belongs to a separate software repository under the author's personal-projects pillar and is explicitly outside the scope of EEEM004. Phase~A of that roadmap (shadow paper-trading with a non-inferiority criterion against the matched-window backtest) is the first natural step; the design is recorded in the repository's deployment README rather than in this dissertation.

\subsection{Group 3 --- Beyond the scope of this dissertation}
\label{sec:group_3_beyond}

\paragraph{Alternative uncertainty estimators.} Compare the Gaussian-NLL head and the Monte-Carlo dropout head used in this dissertation against a deep ensemble~\parencite{lakshminarayanan2017simple} and a conformal-prediction overlay~\parencite{vovk2005algorithmic}.

\paragraph{Risk-aware reward shaping.} Compare the implicit risk control from the trade-scaling factor against an explicit risk-aware reward --- for instance log-growth penalised by drawdown, or a Conditional Value-at-Risk shaped reward~\parencite{rockafellar2000optimization}.

\paragraph{Intraday extension.} Re-calibrate the trade-size scaling and the risk-on guard for minute or tick granularity. This requires a different data source and a different transaction-cost model.

\paragraph{Multi-asset portfolio optimisation.} The present dissertation runs one agent per ticker. A multi-asset agent that allocates across the market sample jointly is a natural follow-up but requires a different action space and reward structure.

\paragraph{Closing remark.} The dissertation's contribution is deliberately narrow: one design choice, one reproducible protocol, three experimental arms, a SPY two-piece ablation, and 2,097 completed experiment cells. The Phase-2 results confirm the hypothesis that a calibrated uncertainty signal enables a deep reinforcement-learning agent to earn materially higher risk-adjusted returns than the same agent without the signal; the ablation refines that claim by showing that the drawdown-control property specifically requires the full two-piece design. The walk-forward validation across four independent time windows confirms that this is a general finding, not an artefact of the test-window choice. The evidence is honest: six cells failed, the baseline agent's conservatism is a specific outcome of this reward function, and all numbers are backtest-only. Within these bounds, the result is clear and strong.
