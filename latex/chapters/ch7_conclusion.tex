\chapter{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{ch:conclusion}

\section{Summary}
\label{sec:summary}

This dissertation set out to answer one question: does feeding a deep reinforcement-learning trading agent the predictive uncertainty produced by its own forecaster improve the agent's risk-adjusted performance? The answer, based on the Phase-2 evidence reported in Chapter~\ref{ch:results}, is yes --- and the improvement is large, consistent, and robust.

\paragraph{What was built.} The agent is a PPO~\parencite{schulman2017ppo} policy paired with a probabilistic LSTM forecaster~\parencite{hochreiter1997lstm,salinas2020deepar}. The forecaster emits a mean and a spread per step; the spread is normalised to a unit-interval uncertainty score and enters the policy in two places --- as a state coordinate and as a hard guard on new long-side trades. The single piece of mathematics that distinguishes the candidate from a baseline PPO is Equation~\ref{eq:probabilistic_trade_size}. The design was tested in two uncertainty modes: aleatoric (data noise) and epistemic (model unfamiliarity via Monte-Carlo dropout~\parencite{gal2016dropout}).

\paragraph{What was found --- Phase-2 headline.} The full Phase-2 grid trained each agent variant on 70 stocks with 10 random seeds and 50,000 PPO time-steps per cell, producing 2,097 completed cells across three experimental arms. The results are unambiguous:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item The probabilistic agents (both aleatoric and epistemic) grew a \$1\,M starting portfolio to a \emph{median} of \$1.6\,M across the 70-stock universe on the 2022--2025 test window, with a median Sharpe ratio above $+0.68$ and a win-rate (cells finishing above \$1\,M) of 89--92\,\%.
  \item The baseline PPO, without the uncertainty signal, finished at a median of \$999\,063 --- essentially flat --- with a median Sharpe of $-0.04$ and a win-rate of 46\,\%. The baseline agent learned to do almost nothing, because without a confidence signal it could not distinguish dangerous days from safe ones.
  \item The two uncertainty modes (aleatoric and epistemic) produced nearly identical headline numbers. The source of the uncertainty matters less than the presence of any calibrated uncertainty signal.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Walk-forward confirmation.} The walk-forward validation tested the agent on four independent two-year windows covering 2018--2025 (including the COVID crash and the 2022 inflation shock). Across 320 walk-forward cells, the probabilistic agent achieved a median final value of \$1,167,050, a median Sharpe of $+0.55$, and a win-rate of 87.2\,\% --- compared to the baseline's \$997,591, $-0.10$, and 46.9\,\%. The advantage held on every single fold. This is strong evidence that the agent learned a general strategy, not a pattern specific to one test period.

\paragraph{What was tested but did not change the headline.} The epistemic arm (Arm~C) was added in response to project-proposal feedback. At full Phase-2 scale, its median final value (\$1,619,713) and win-rate (92.3\,\%) are within noise of the aleatoric arm (\$1,612,478, 89.1\,\%). The dissertation reports both honestly. The practical implication is that a practitioner can use either uncertainty source with similar expected outcomes.

\paragraph{What is not claimed.} The dissertation does not propose a new reinforcement-learning algorithm; PPO is unchanged. It does not propose a new forecasting architecture; the probabilistic LSTM is a 2020 idea built on a 1997 idea. It does not claim multi-asset portfolio optimisation; one agent runs per ticker. It does not claim live-trading performance; all evidence is backtest evidence on adjusted-close daily data. It does not claim the design works in every regime; Section~\ref{sec:wins_and_losses} names regimes (sustained low-uncertainty bull markets and very-low-drawdown defensives) in which it under-performs and explains why. Six out of 2,100 cells failed (0.29\,\%), all on a single small-cap biotech ticker (RYTM), due to a numerical edge case in the Gaussian loss.

\section{Future work}
\label{sec:future_work}

The future-work items are split into three groups. Group~1 is work that can be completed before the September 2026 submission. Group~2 is a real-world deployment roadmap. Group~3 is post-dissertation research extensions.

\subsection{Group 1 --- Before submission}
\label{sec:group_1_scheduled}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{Sector-aware uncertainty calibration.} Replace the single global uncertainty-guard threshold ($\tau = 0.80$) with per-sector or per-regime thresholds calibrated on the validation window. Section~\ref{sec:wins_and_losses} identifies persistent-trend single names as the regime where the global threshold costs the most; this is the most surgical fix.

  \item \textbf{Ablation study.} Compare four PPO variants on the same protocol: pure baseline (no uncertainty), uncertainty as a state feature only (no guard), uncertainty guard only (no state feature), and the full design that has both. The aim is to attribute how much of the headline result comes from each of the two pieces.

  \item \textbf{Sensitivity sweep.} Sweep the uncertainty quantile threshold over $\{0.7, 0.8, 0.9\}$, the minimum scale $s_{\min}$ over $\{0.05, 0.10, 0.20\}$, and the maximum trade fraction $f_{\max}$ over $\{0.05, 0.10, 0.20\}$. Report the headline metrics over the resulting 27-cell grid on SPY.

  \item \textbf{Bootstrap-augmented training.} Generate synthetic training paths by block-bootstrap resampling of historical return sequences~\parencite{politis1994stationary} to expand the effective training set without leaving the empirical return distribution.

  \item \textbf{Shock-window case studies.} Score the agents on the protocol shock periods (COVID 2020, Ukraine-war onset 2022) as standalone case studies, with per-window equity curves and metric tables.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Group 2 --- Real-world deployment roadmap (post-submission)}
\label{sec:group_2_deployment}

The dissertation answers a scientific question about a design choice. A separate engineering question is whether the resulting agent can be made useful against a real, live portfolio. The roadmap below phases that work from low risk to higher risk so that each phase produces evidence the next phase needs.

\paragraph{Phase A --- Shadow paper-trading via Alpaca.}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item Provision an Alpaca paper-trading account and store credentials in environment variables.
  \item Wire the saved policy and forecaster behind a daily polling loop.
  \item Run in shadow mode for at least two weeks of trading days; the agent acts only on the paper account.
  \item Reconcile the live equity curve against a simultaneous backtest replay to confirm the live wiring reproduces the backtest behaviour up to fill-time and slippage.
  \item Go/no-go criterion for Phase B: the live capital-preservation ratio sits above 0.95 and the live Sharpe is non-inferior to the matched-window backtest.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Phase B --- Decision-support advisor.}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item Pull current positions and prices, run the agent on the day's observation, and emit a recommendation by email or Slack.
  \item Operate as decision-support only --- the human is the executor, the agent is the advisor --- for at least one full quarter.
  \item Maintain a side-by-side ledger that records what the agent suggested, what the human did, and the resulting profit-and-loss attribution.
  \item Go/no-go criterion for Phase C: across the quarter the recommendations would have produced a Sharpe non-inferior to the actual portfolio.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Phase C --- Scheduled execution with continuous risk gates.}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item A cron-driven entry point that runs the advisor and, where it satisfies pre-trade risk gates, places orders on the live account.
  \item Pre-trade risk gates are non-negotiable: position-size cap, gross-exposure cap, sector-exposure cap, daily-loss cap, and a circuit breaker tied to the high-watermark drawdown threshold.
  \item Run only on a small fraction of equity initially; size up only after each successive month continues to satisfy the preservation and Sharpe gates.
  \item Stop criterion: any breach of the preservation gate halts the executor and forces a return to Phase B.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Group 3 --- Beyond the scope of this dissertation}
\label{sec:group_3_beyond}

\paragraph{Alternative uncertainty estimators.} Compare the Gaussian-NLL head and the Monte-Carlo dropout head used in this dissertation against a deep ensemble~\parencite{lakshminarayanan2017simple} and a conformal-prediction overlay~\parencite{vovk2005algorithmic}.

\paragraph{Risk-aware reward shaping.} Compare the implicit risk control from the trade-scaling factor against an explicit risk-aware reward --- for instance log-growth penalised by drawdown, or a Conditional Value-at-Risk shaped reward~\parencite{rockafellar2000optimization}.

\paragraph{Intraday extension.} Re-calibrate the trade-size scaling and the risk-on guard for minute or tick granularity. This requires a different data source and a different transaction-cost model.

\paragraph{Multi-asset portfolio optimisation.} The present dissertation runs one agent per ticker. A multi-asset agent that allocates across the market sample jointly is a natural follow-up but requires a different action space and reward structure.

\paragraph{Closing remark.} The dissertation's contribution is deliberately narrow: one design choice, one reproducible protocol, three experimental arms, and 2,097 completed experiment cells. The Phase-2 results confirm the hypothesis that a calibrated uncertainty signal enables a deep reinforcement-learning agent to earn materially higher risk-adjusted returns than the same agent without the signal. The walk-forward validation across four independent time windows confirms that this is a general finding, not an artefact of the test-window choice. The evidence is honest: six cells failed, the baseline agent's conservatism is a specific outcome of this reward function, and all numbers are backtest-only. Within these bounds, the result is clear and strong.
