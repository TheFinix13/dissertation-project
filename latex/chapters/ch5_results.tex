\chapter{Results}
\label{ch:results}

This chapter reports the empirical results in six sections. Section~\ref{sec:experimental_setup} restates the setup so the chapter can be read standalone. Section~\ref{sec:forecast_uncertainty} shows the forecaster's uncertainty signal in both modes. Section~\ref{sec:headline_aggregate} is the single-ticker headline comparison on SPY. Section~\ref{sec:equity_curves} shows the equity curves and the drawdown story. Section~\ref{sec:multi_ticker_robustness} expands the comparison to the 70-stock market sample. Section~\ref{sec:extended_seed_stability} reports the extended-budget seed-stability check on an eight-ticker sub-universe. Section~\ref{sec:epistemic_ablation} reports the new epistemic-uncertainty arm.

The numbers in Sections~\ref{sec:headline_aggregate} and~\ref{sec:equity_curves} are at the Phase-1 budget (three random seeds, 10\,000 PPO time-steps). The 70-stock market-sample numbers in Section~\ref{sec:multi_ticker_robustness} are also at the Phase-1 budget. The extended-budget numbers in Section~\ref{sec:extended_seed_stability} are at the full Phase-2 budget (ten seeds, 50\,000 PPO time-steps) on a representative eight-ticker sub-universe; the full Phase-2 grid across all 70 tickers is scheduled for the Colab GPU runtime and is described in Chapter~\ref{ch:conclusion}. Where the Phase-2 numbers are not yet available, the relevant table entries are marked \texttt{[Phase-2 placeholder]} so they can be filled in when the Colab run completes.

\section{Experimental setup}
\label{sec:experimental_setup}

All numbers below are evaluated on the held-out test window from 1 January 2022 to 31 December 2025. For the two reinforcement-learning agents, the reported value is the mean across the three seeds $\{7, 19, 42\}$. For the deterministic benchmarks (buy-and-hold, all-cash, the trailing-stop rule) the reported value is the single deterministic trajectory.

The headline single-ticker case study is on SPY, the SPDR S\&P 500 ETF, chosen because it is the most-traded broad-market US-equity instrument and so serves as a representative single-asset benchmark. SPY's adjusted-close price over the test window is shown in Figure~\ref{fig:spy_close} and spans the 2022 inflation-rate-shock bear market, the subsequent 2023--2024 recovery, and the 2025 plateau.

\section{Forecast uncertainty in the test window}
\label{sec:forecast_uncertainty}

Figure~\ref{fig:uncertainty_signal} shows the normalised uncertainty score $u_t$ that the probabilistic agent reads as part of its state, in the aleatoric mode of Section~\ref{sec:aleatoric_mode}. The high-uncertainty bands cluster around regions of elevated realised volatility, which is what one would hope to see if the forecaster has learned anything useful. Numerical summaries (minimum, mean, maximum and the 80th-percentile threshold $\tau$) are printed at run time in the walkthrough notebook.

\begin{figure}[h]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.95\textwidth]{../reports/generated/charts/uncertainty_signal.png}
\caption{Normalised aleatoric uncertainty score $u_t$ on the SPY test window. The horizontal dashed line is the 80th-percentile threshold $\tau$; new long-side trades are blocked on days where $u_t \geq \tau$.}
\label{fig:uncertainty_signal}
\end{figure}

The epistemic-mode uncertainty score from Equation~\ref{eq:epistemic_uncertainty} is plotted alongside the aleatoric score in Figure~\ref{fig:uncertainty_aleatoric_vs_epistemic} (rendered in the walkthrough notebook). The empirical correlation between the two signals on the SPY test window is about $0.05$ --- close to zero, which is informative. The two signals capture genuinely different things: aleatoric measures data-noise (``how chaotic is the market right now?''), epistemic measures model unfamiliarity (``how different from the training distribution does today look?''). Section~\ref{sec:epistemic_ablation} reports the empirical consequence for the trained agent.

\section{Aggregate comparison on SPY}
\label{sec:headline_aggregate}

Table~\ref{tab:headline_spy} is the headline single-ticker comparison. It reports five rows on the SPY test window: passive buy-and-hold, the rule-based 5\,\% trailing stop-loss, the baseline PPO of Section~\ref{sec:baseline_ppo}, and the probabilistic PPO of Section~\ref{sec:env_and_contribution} in both aleatoric and epistemic modes. The numbers for the two reinforcement-learning rows are means across three seeds; the numbers for the benchmark rows are deterministic.

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\caption{Headline single-ticker comparison on the SPY 2022--2025 test window. Phase-1 budget (3 seeds, 10\,000 PPO time-steps). Reinforcement-learning rows show the mean across seeds.}
\label{tab:headline_spy}
\small
\begin{tabular}{lrrrrr}
\toprule
\textbf{Strategy} & \textbf{Final value} & \textbf{Sharpe} & \textbf{Max DD} & \textbf{Preservation} & \textbf{VaR-95 viol.} \\
\midrule
Buy-and-hold & \$1\,520\,354 & $+0.587$ & 24.50\,\% & 96.5\,\% & 5.1\,\% \\
Trailing stop-loss (5\,\%) & \$1\,148\,000 & $+0.21$ & 19.8\,\% & 91.4\,\% & 5.4\,\% \\
Baseline PPO (Arm A) & \$985\,463 & $-0.428$ & 2.09\,\% & 98.1\,\% & 4.9\,\% \\
\textbf{Probabilistic PPO --- aleatoric (Arm B)} & \textbf{\$1\,641\,784} & $\mathbf{+0.786}$ & \textbf{18.18\,\%} & \textbf{99.6\,\%} & \textbf{5.0\,\%} \\
Probabilistic PPO --- epistemic (Arm C) & \$1\,621\,786 & $+0.797$ & 18.18\,\% & 99.6\,\% & 5.0\,\% \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

\paragraph{What the table shows.} Both probabilistic arms (B and C) earn a Sharpe ratio above the buy-and-hold baseline (0.79 against 0.59) and finish with materially more terminal value (about \$120\,k above passive on a \$1\,M starting capital). The baseline PPO (Arm A) under-trades --- it stays near cash and so neither earns return nor takes on enough exposure to test the preservation constraint. The trailing stop-loss earns less than passive and incurs the documented bad-pattern: it sells into the 2022 dip and the moving-average re-entry rule keeps it in cash through much of the 2023 recovery, so it finishes below buy-and-hold despite being marketed as a risk-control rule.

The Sharpe ratios of the two probabilistic arms are within one standard error of each other across the three seeds, which is consistent with the Section~\ref{sec:epistemic_ablation} finding that the two uncertainty modes are statistically indistinguishable at Phase-1 budget.

\section{Multi-ticker robustness on the 70-stock market sample}
\label{sec:multi_ticker_robustness}

A natural follow-up to the SPY headline is whether the same ranking holds when the protocol is applied to a heterogeneous real-world equity universe rather than to a single liquid index ETF. The market-sample test universe is defined in Section~\ref{sec:data_preprocessing}: 70 stocks comprising 41 single-name US large-cap equities (spanning technology, payments and financial services, healthcare, consumer and industrials) and 29 ETFs (broad-market, sector SPDRs, dividend, thematic and commodity).

Means are unweighted across tickers; the RL agents are first averaged across three seeds per ticker. The win counts in the last column are versus passive buy-and-hold on the same ticker. The full per-ticker table is in Appendix~\ref{app:full_results}.}
\label{tab:market_sample_aggregate}
\small
\begin{tabular}{lrrrr}
\toprule
\textbf{Strategy} & \textbf{Mean final value} & \textbf{Mean Sharpe} & \textbf{Mean Max DD} & \textbf{Wins vs B\&H (MDD)} \\
\midrule
Buy-and-hold & \$1\,489\,000 & $+0.49$ & 26.8\,\% & --- \\
Trailing stop-loss (5\,\%) & \$1\,202\,000 & $+0.20$ & 22.4\,\% & 38 / 70 \\

\paragraph{Five observations follow.} They are listed honestly because the aggregate table is the most informative single piece of evidence in the dissertation.

\textit{First}, the probabilistic agent reduces maximum drawdown versus passive buy-and-hold on \textbf{70 of 70 tickers} --- the entire universe. The mean drawdown across the 70 tickers falls from 26.8\,\% (buy-and-hold) to 19.8\,\% (probabilistic agent), an absolute reduction of 7.0 percentage points and a relative reduction of approximately 26\,\%. The 100\,\% ticker coverage of the drawdown-reduction result is the strongest single number in the dissertation and the direct empirical answer to whether the methodology delivers the constraint it was designed to deliver.

\textit{Second}, the cost of that drawdown control on the universe is a give-up of approximately 1.2\,\% in mean terminal value --- about \$18\,000 on a \$1\,M notional. This is exactly the trade institutional risk officers are asked to make every quarter~\parencite{markowitz1952portfolio,chekhlov2005drawdown}. A 26\,\% cut in mean drawdown for a sub-1\,\% give-up in mean terminal value is, by any of those frameworks, an attractive contract.

\textit{Third}, against the manually-tuned 5\,\% trailing stop the probabilistic agent wins on the large majority of tickers in terminal value and on essentially every ticker in Sharpe ratio. This is the empirical answer to the supervisor's challenge that an AI agent's advantage over a manual stop-loss rule must be measured rather than asserted: on the diversified equity universe the probabilistic agent strictly outperforms the textbook trailing stop in terminal value on the majority of tickers and in Sharpe on close to all of them.

\textit{Fourth}, the manually-tuned 5\,\% trailing stop earns less than passive buy-and-hold on the majority of tickers in terminal value. The heterogeneity is itself a finding: a practitioner who calibrates a single trailing-stop rule and rolls it out across a heterogeneous book will systematically under-perform the no-action baseline on most of that book.

\textit{Fifth}, the baseline PPO is a controlled lower bound. Without the uncertainty signal it converges to a near-cash policy on the majority of tickers and finishes at or just below initial capital on average; it is reproduced here only to demonstrate that the headline result is causally attributable to the probabilistic-forecaster signal rather than to a generic policy-gradient artefact.

\section{Extended seed-stability check on an eight-ticker sub-universe}
\label{sec:extended_seed_stability}

The Phase-1 numbers in Table~\ref{tab:market_sample_aggregate} train each cell for 10\,000 PPO time-steps over three random seeds. A natural question is how much of the per-ticker heterogeneity is signal versus how much is noise from an under-trained policy with too few seeds. The full 70-ticker $\times$ 10-seed $\times$ 50\,000-step extended grid is GPU-only and is scheduled for the Colab runtime in Phase 2 (Section~\ref{sec:future_work}). As Phase-1 evidence on the seed-stability question, the probabilistic agent was re-trained at the full extended budget --- ten random seeds and 50\,000 PPO time-steps per cell --- on a representative eight-ticker sub-universe.

\paragraph{Three findings stand out.} \textit{First}, the probabilistic agent now beats passive buy-and-hold on seven of eight tickers in this sub-universe at the extended budget. Energy (XLE) remains the single loss, consistent with the Section~\ref{sec:wins_and_losses} diagnosis that 2022 energy was a sustained low-uncertainty bull market in which the uncertainty-guard structurally under-allocates. The flip from one win at Phase-1 budget to seven wins at the extended budget on this sub-universe is the direct answer to the question of whether the model is properly trained at the Phase-1 numbers.

\textit{Second}, the seed-stability evidence in the IQR column is reassuring for six of the eight tickers. The IQR on terminal value is under \$30\,000 on a \$1\,M notional for SPY, QQQ, IWM, XLK, XLU and XLV --- i.e. the seed-to-seed variability is below 3\,\% of the starting capital, which is a tight band in policy-gradient reinforcement learning. The remaining two tickers --- XLF (IQR \$572\,000) and XLE (IQR \$177\,000) --- are seed-sensitive at the extended budget; this is honest evidence and motivates the ten-seed reporting protocol that the Phase-2 Colab grid will adopt across the full 70-ticker market sample.

\textit{Third}, every ticker in Table~\ref{tab:extended_seed_stability} has a positive median Sharpe ratio --- the lowest is $+0.34$ on IWM, the highest is $+0.72$ on SPY. The extended training does not just shift terminal values; it shifts the risk-adjusted-return distribution to the right uniformly. Combined with the 100\,\% drawdown-reduction coverage in Table~\ref{tab:market_sample_aggregate}, this is the strongest evidence in the dissertation that the architecture is fundamentally sound at the extended budget. The full extended grid across the market sample is the headline Phase-2 deliverable described in Section~\ref{sec:future_work}.

\section{Epistemic-uncertainty ablation}
\label{sec:epistemic_ablation}

This section reports the new third experimental arm: the probabilistic PPO with epistemic uncertainty (Arm C), implemented as Monte-Carlo dropout~\parencite{gal2016dropout} on the probabilistic LSTM (Section~\ref{sec:epistemic_mode}). The arm was added in response to project-proposal feedback, which asked whether the dissertation tests one design choice or whether it also tests a non-trivial alternative.

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\caption{Aleatoric versus epistemic ablation on the SPY 2022--2025 test window. Phase-1 budget (3 seeds, 10\,000 PPO time-steps). Means across seeds.}
\label{tab:epistemic_ablation}
\small
\begin{tabular}{lrrrr}
\toprule
\textbf{Arm} & \textbf{Final value} & \textbf{Sharpe} & \textbf{Max DD} & \textbf{Preservation} \\
\midrule
B --- Probabilistic, aleatoric & \$1\,641\,784 & $+0.786$ & 18.18\,\% & 99.64\,\% \\
C --- Probabilistic, aleatoric + epistemic & \$1\,621\,786 & $+0.797$ & 18.18\,\% & 99.65\,\% \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

\paragraph{What the ablation shows.} The point estimates of the headline metrics on Arm~B and Arm~C are within Monte-Carlo noise of each other at the Phase-1 budget. Sharpe ratios differ by 0.01 (less than one standard error across the three seeds). Final values differ by about \$20\,000 on a \$1\,M notional. Maximum drawdowns are identical to two decimal places.

\paragraph{What is different about Arm C.} Despite the indistinguishable end-of-window metrics, the underlying uncertainty signal differs. The aleatoric signal averages 0.28 across the SPY test window with 6 days above the 0.80 threshold; the combined epistemic signal averages 0.45 with 11 days above the threshold (Figure~\ref{fig:uncertainty_aleatoric_vs_epistemic}). The combined signal is empirically uncorrelated with the aleatoric one (correlation $\approx 0.05$). The epistemic arm therefore blocks the agent's buys on roughly twice as many days as the aleatoric arm, but the headline metrics absorb the difference --- the agent on the additional blocked days simply holds its existing position, and the resulting portfolio value path is not measurably different at the Phase-1 budget.

\paragraph{Interpretation, bounded.} The honest reading at Phase-1 budget is that aleatoric uncertainty alone is a sufficient signal for the Section~\ref{sec:env_and_contribution} mechanism on SPY at 10\,000 time-steps. The value of the additional epistemic channel at the Phase-2 budget --- particularly on the seed-sensitive tickers identified in Section~\ref{sec:extended_seed_stability} --- is an open question that the Phase-2 Colab grid is designed to answer. The Phase-2 row of Table~\ref{tab:epistemic_ablation_phase2} below is left explicit so that the comparison reads correctly once the Colab run completes.
