# Gates 2 to 4 audit for the post-viva experiments

Written 29 September 2026. This document covers Gates 2, 3 and 4 of the
literature-first protocol for Track B steps 3 to 5 of the improvement
plan: fair tuning, more seeds, and a more realistic simulator. It
measures the data and the code before any new training, and it is
meant to answer an examiner's "how do you know?" for each design
choice in `2026-09-29-experiment-designs.md`. Gate 1 is the literature
review in `lit-review-right-data.md`, which gained Themes F and G for
this work.

Every number here can be regenerated. The data measurements come from
`experiments/final_v2/gate_audit.py` (writes `results/gate_audit.json`,
about 5 seconds, no training). The learner checks come from
`test_learners.py`, `ppo_grad_diag.py` (writes
`results/ppo_grad_diag.json`, about 50 seconds) and
`run_ppo_scale_check.py` (writes `results/ppo_scale_check.json` and
`results/scale_check_*.json`). No simulated test month and no new real
month was used. The first draft was reviewed by a GPT model on 29
September (`.agent-reviews/2026-09-29_gates-and-designs.md`), and its
three blocking objections are answered in this version.

## Headline

The dissertation's PPO result is consistent with an optimisation-scale
failure, and the measurements below point to one specific cause. PPO
received rewards in dollars. Stable-Baselines3 (SB3) adds the policy
loss to half the value loss, clips the joint gradient to a norm of 0.5
and takes one Adam step. On the simulated training months, the value
gradient's norm was about 372 against 0.2 for the policy, so clipping
scaled every gradient by about 0.0014. That left the policy's gradient
elements near 4e-7, below Adam's epsilon of 1e-5, and the policy's
weights moved four times less than with scaled rewards. The policy
drifted to holding with probability 0.82 whatever the month.

On the simulated validation months, the same PPO with rewards divided
by the starting capital earned +$153, +$131 and +$78 per month on three
seeds, and all three policies responded to the market state. With
dollar rewards, all three did nothing, as in the dissertation. On the
same months, DQN at its dissertation settings earned +$150, +$177 and
+$175, and buy-and-hold earned +$5.66. DQN therefore stays ahead on
these three seeds, but its lead over PPO falls from $167 to $46 per
month, and PPO here still runs on every other default.

This changes how Chapter 5's PPO result must be read, and it means the
fair-tuning step is necessary rather than optional. The rest of this
document records the data measurements that shape the other two steps.

## Gate 2: the data

### Volume against capacity

A simulated month gives the agent 20 decisions. The 3,000 training
months therefore hold 60,000 distinct decisions. DQN and REINFORCE each
train one network of 2,819 weights, so they have 21 decisions per
weight and about one independent month per weight. PPO trains a policy
network and a separate value network, 5,572 weights in total, so it has
11 decisions per weight and half a month per weight. The 300,000-step
budget passes over the training set five times.

The same budget does not buy the same amount of learning. DQN makes a
gradient update at almost every step, 299,000 updates in total. PPO
collects 2,048 steps and then makes 320 minibatch updates, 47,040 in
total. REINFORCE updates once per month, 15,000 times. Equal steps in
the environment is the standard budget in reinforcement learning, but
the tuning must let each method choose how much it updates per step.

The generator can produce unlimited months, so data volume is a choice
and not a constraint. Generating a fresh month for every training
episode would give 300,000 distinct decisions, about 106 per DQN
weight. The tuning step keeps the dissertation's 3,000 months so that
only one thing changes at a time, and the simulator step revisits it.

### Balance

On the simulated test set, doing nothing earns $0 per month and
buy-and-hold loses $60.29. The regimes are balanced, and a falling month
loses more ($710) than a rising month gains ($510). A strategy that knew
the regime and went fully in or fully out at the first bar would earn
$176.39 per month. DQN earns $126.94, which is 72% of that ceiling. In
rising months it keeps $474 of the $510 available, and in falling
months it loses $103 where buy-and-hold loses $710.

The real months are not balanced. Of the 144 consecutive months from
2006 to 2017, 52 rose by more than 2%, 29 fell by more than 2% and 63
were flat. Buy-and-hold earns +$80.24 per month over the 180 transfer
months. The best constant strategy is therefore cash in the simulator
but buy-and-hold in the real market. The agent learns in a world where
staying out is the safe default and is tested in one where staying in
is. That alone predicts the lower real-market exposure (0.71) reported
in the statistics note.

Cash in the environment earns nothing, which flatters staying out.
Treasury bills paid between about 0% and 5% a year over the transfer
months, which is up to about $40 a month on $10,000. The environment
also ignores bid-ask spreads, market impact and tax. The real-market
comparisons in the design document therefore add a Treasury-bill
benchmark.

### Signal content

The worked example is the first bar of a month. The agent sees five
market features: one-day return, five-day momentum, five-day
volatility, gap to the 20-day average and normalised range. Can a
linear discriminant tell from these what kind of month it will be?

In the simulator the regime that generated the month is recovered 77%
of the time at bar 0, against 33% for always guessing one regime. That
is the hidden state being close to observed. The month's realised label
(up more than 2%, down more than 2%, or flat) is predicted 57.5% of the
time, against 40.8% for the training set's most common label. The two
differ because a month drawn from the up regime can still end flat.

In the real market there is no generating regime to recover, only the
realised label. A classifier fitted on the 60 calibration months and
tested on the 180 transfer months is right 38.3% of the time, against
40.6% for the most common label. Accuracy rises to about 70% by the
fifteenth bar in both worlds, but from bar 1 onward the features
contain part of the month's own return, so that rise is not a forecast.

The same result holds for the quantity the agent needs, the return
over the rest of the month. A linear regression on the five features
has an out-of-sample R squared of 0.23 at the first bar in the
simulator. On real months it is negative at every bar tested (−0.15 at
bar 0, −0.06 at bar 5, −0.22 at bar 10, −0.41 at bar 15). The 95%
block-bootstrap interval at bar 0 runs from −0.41 to +0.01. Both real
rows are scored against the calibration months' mean return. This
matches what Welch and Goyal (2008) report for monthly predictors.

The careful reading is narrow. These five features gave no linear
out-of-sample forecast of direction on real months, while in the
simulator they did, because the generator puts the regime into them.
Non-linear models were not tested, and 60 calibration months is a small
fitting set. The measurement is still the examiners' point in numbers:
what the agent sees in the simulator is not what it sees in the market.

The cause in the simulator is the warm-up history. `sim_data.py`
generates the 20-plus warm-up days in the same regime as the month, so
the features at bar 0 already describe that regime. In real data the
warm-up is the previous month, and the direction of one month barely
predicts the next. The chance that a rising month follows a rising
month is 0.38, close to the 0.36 share of rising months overall. The
volatility of one month, however, predicts the next strongly, with a
month-to-month autocorrelation of 0.72. In the real market the
direction is close to unpredictable, while the volatility state is very
predictable. This matches Theme B (Moreira and Muir 2017) and Theme F
(Ang and Timmermann 2012) of the review.

### Realism

The generator draws daily returns from a normal distribution within
each regime. The table compares its daily returns with the 180 real
transfer months.

| Property | Simulator | Real SPY |
|---|---|---|
| Excess kurtosis of daily returns | 1.24 | 15.89 |
| Lag-1 autocorrelation of absolute returns | 0.15 | 0.30 |
| Lag-1 autocorrelation of returns | 0.02 | −0.08 |
| Months whose two halves move in opposite directions | 40% | 53% |
| Month-to-month autocorrelation of volatility | 0 by construction | 0.72 |

Real returns have much fatter tails, stronger volatility clustering,
and more reversals inside a month. The simulator's clustering comes
only from mixing two volatility levels across episodes. Rydén et al.
(1998) and Nystrup et al. (2015) show that a hidden Markov model with
persistent states reproduces most of these properties, which gives the
simulator step a tested starting point.

### Coverage

A uniform random policy over the legal actions visits every reachable
combination of bar and exposure level, 90 cells in total, at least 51
times in 3,000 months. Exploration from a random start therefore
reaches every reachable account position at every bar. It says nothing
about coverage of the continuous market features, which were not
binned. The collapse to doing nothing is not a gap in account-state
coverage. It happens when a policy moves away from random behaviour,
which is where the entropy setting and the reward-scale fault act.

## Gate 3: the implementation

### What the tests already covered

The 21 existing tests in `test_v2.py` pass. They cover the features
(no future data, no boundary truncation), the accounting identity, the
reward, the action mask and the data split. None of them checked that a
learner can solve a problem with a known answer, so a learner fault
could pass every test.

### Known-answer probe

The probe world has two kinds of month. Rising months gain 1% a day and
falling months lose 1% a day, and the momentum feature says which kind
the month is. The right policy buys in rising months (+$2,021) and
holds cash in falling ones ($0). Each run used 40,000 steps.

| Learner and setting | Seeds solved |
|---|---|
| DQN, dissertation settings | 3 of 3 (one seed lost $76 in falling months) |
| REINFORCE, dissertation settings | 2 of 3 (the third never bought) |
| PPO, SB3 defaults | 0 of 3 (bought in every month, −$1,697 when falling) |
| PPO, defaults with 150,000 steps | 0 of 2 |
| PPO, entropy coefficient 0.01 | 0 of 2 |
| PPO, reward divided by $10,000 | 2 of 2 |
| PPO, gradient clipping removed | 2 of 2 |

More steps and more entropy did not help. Either scaling the reward or
removing the clipping fixed it. The installed SB3 source
(`sb3_contrib/ppo_mask/ppo_mask.py`) confirms one combined loss, one
`clip_grad_norm_` over all parameters and one Adam step. Adam removes a
constant gradient scale, so clipping alone should not stop learning. It
does stop learning once the clipped gradient falls below Adam's
epsilon, and `ppo_grad_diag.py` measured exactly that.

| Median over rollouts | Toy, dollars | Toy, scaled | Simulator, dollars | Simulator, scaled |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Policy gradient norm | 0.15 | 0.12 | 0.20 | 0.19 |
| Value gradient norm | 2,200 | 0.003 | 372 | 0.004 |
| Clip factor | 0.00023 | 1 | 0.0014 | 1 |
| Median clipped policy gradient element | 5e-8 | 2e-4 | 4e-7 | 2e-4 |
| Actor weight change per rollout | 0.036 | 0.58 | 0.093 | 0.39 |

With dollar rewards the policy's gradient sat 20 to 200 times below
Adam's epsilon of 1e-5, and its weights moved 4 to 16 times less. At
the first bar of the toy world, the dollar-reward policy chose Buy with
probability 0.60 in rising and 0.59 in falling months. The scaled
policy chose Buy with probability 0.99 in rising months and Hold with
0.996 in falling ones.

This also answers a fair objection about evaluation. The dissertation
scored PPO greedily. A policy that has barely moved from its starting
weights is close to indifferent, and greedy scoring turns that into a
fixed corner, always buying in the toy world and always holding in the
simulator. The collapse is real, but it is a policy that never learned
rather than one that learned to stay out.

The scratch agents were not affected. DQN has no gradient clipping, and
REINFORCE standardises its returns within each episode. A test confirms
that REINFORCE is invariant to the reward scale: after 20,000 steps with
dollar and scaled rewards it made identical trades.

The probe is now `test_learners.py`, five tests in about 45 seconds.
Two check that DQN and scaled PPO solve the world, and one documents the
raw-dollar PPO failure. One checks that the reward scaling leaves the
dollar accounting unchanged, and one checks REINFORCE's invariance to
the reward scale. `harness.train_one` gained a
`reward_scale` path for PPO and an `algo_kwargs` argument for the
tuning search, and `agents.train_reinforce` gained an entropy
coefficient. Leaving all three at their defaults reproduces the
dissertation runs exactly.

On the simulated validation months, REINFORCE at its dissertation
settings collapsed as before. Two seeds never traded and the third
earned +$0.90 per month without responding to the state.

### Settings nobody chose

Every hyperparameter in the main study was a default. PPO used SB3
2.9.0's defaults apart from the network size: learning rate 3e-4, 2,048
steps per rollout, minibatch 64, 10 epochs, GAE lambda 0.95, clip 0.2,
entropy coefficient 0, value coefficient 0.5, gradient clip 0.5 and no
reward normalisation. DQN used the defaults written in `agents.py`:
learning rate 1e-3, replay buffer 50,000, batch 64, target sync every
500 steps, exploration decaying from 1.0 to 0.05 over half of training.
REINFORCE used learning rate 1e-3 and has no entropy term. The only
tuning in the project was the reward-scale test in
`run_conditioning.py`, which ran on real validation months and covered
DQN and REINFORCE but not PPO.

### Held-out data

The simulated validation months were used for selection in the
dissertation (the reward and feature cells in `run_sim_suite.py` and
the stop-loss tuning), and the simulated test months have been reported.
Neither is clean any more. Because the generator is unlimited, the new
experiments draw a fresh tuning set and a fresh test set with new seeds.
The test set is generated once and scored once, at the end. The 180
real transfer months have also been read many times, so real-market
results from the new runs are re-analyses. A clean real hold-out needs
assets or periods named before any run, which the design document does.

## Gate 4: compatibility

### Capacity against data

At 21 decisions per weight for DQN and 11 for PPO, the networks are
small for the data but not tiny. The tuning keeps the 64-32 network for
all three methods, so capacity is not a difference between them. The
simulator step then tests whether fresh months (106 decisions per
weight) change the ranking.

### Exploration against the data's attractors

Doing nothing is the locally safe action in the simulator, because cash
is the best constant strategy there. A policy with no entropy bonus can
settle on it early. The tuning therefore searches the entropy
coefficient for both policy-gradient methods (0, 1e-4, 1e-3 and 1e-2 for
PPO as the review specified, and the same range for REINFORCE, which
needs an entropy term added). This follows Dr Nguyen's grouping. PPO and
REINFORCE share one mechanism, so they get the same exploration
controls, while DQN keeps its epsilon schedule.

### Decision frequency against information

The features change daily, but in the real market they carry no
forecast of the rest of the month and no forecast of the next month's
direction. They do forecast volatility. A daily trading decision based
on direction therefore has little to work with in real data, however
well it is tuned. The honest prediction for the tuning step is that the
simulated ranking may change while the real-market result stays
indistinguishable from exposure-matched buy-and-hold. The
domain-first study (Track C) is where the inputs change.

### Seeds against months

On the real months, almost all the uncertainty in DQN's gap to
buy-and-hold comes from which months were sampled, not from which seed
was trained. A two-way bootstrap re-estimates the whole mean each time,
drawing seeds and then 6-month blocks inside each window. With 6 seeds
the 95% interval half-width is $43.1 per month. Holding the six seeds
fixed and resampling months alone gives $43.5, which is the same within
bootstrap noise. Projected to 20 seeds, drawn from the six trained ones,
it is $42.3. For PPO the months-only width is 96% of the 6-seed width,
and for REINFORCE it is 79%, because its seeds split between collapsed
and active policies. The 20-seed figure is a projection, because six
seeds cannot show the full spread that 20 would.

Twenty seeds are still worth running. They measure how often each
method collapses, and they tighten the simulated comparisons, where the
test set has 600 months. They cannot make the real-market claim
significant. That needs more real months, for example SPY before 2006
or other markets, or a different question.

## What the gates decide

The three steps can go ahead in this order, as set out in
`2026-09-29-experiment-designs.md`. Fair tuning comes first, with the
reward scale as a searched setting for all three methods and fresh
tuning and test sets. The 20-seed run follows on the selected settings.
The simulator redesign comes last. Its acceptance test is the Gate 2
measurements above, so a new generator must match real data on signal
content as well as on the return statistics before any agent trains on
it.
